# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **A link you open from a tab lands under that tab again after you drag or close a link.** Links opened from a tab stack under it in the order opened. Before, if you dragged the last of them somewhere else (to the bottom of the list, say), the next link went next to the dragged one instead of under the tab; and if you dragged it under another tab and closed it, the next link went under that other tab. Now a link only counts as the last one while it still sits in the run of links directly under the tab, so the next link goes under the tab, or after the links still there.
- **Staying in your space after Clear, and in two more cases.** After Clear, closing the tab you are on (a pinned tab, say) no longer lands on one of the cleared tabs that are still open in the 8 seconds Undo is on offer. You stay in your space with the next tab down the list, or a New Tab if there is none. If you pick a cleared tab on purpose in Chrome's own tab strip or with Cmd+1 to 9, that one tab comes back into the list and leaves Archived, and the others still close when the 8 seconds end. Closing an empty New Tab that sits among other tabs of a space also keeps you in that space instead of following Chrome to a tab of another space. Closing the only tab of an empty space is unchanged. With favorites shared between spaces (the default), the most recently used tab shortcut pressed on a favorite from another space now stays in the space you are in instead of jumping into the favorite's space.
- **Clear's Undo window no longer closes a tab you pinned, or brings back tabs when a window closes.** If you pin a cleared tab from Chrome's own tab strip in the 8 seconds after Clear, Sidecove now keeps it open and shows it with your pinned tabs, instead of closing it when the time is up; Undo puts it with the pinned tabs too. If you close the window in those 8 seconds, the cleared tabs are no longer listed to come back into their space when you reopen the window from History or with Cmd+Shift+T. Quitting Chrome or an extension update inside the 8 seconds is not covered yet. Also, if Chrome stops Sidecove's background worker at the very moment the 8 seconds end, the cleared tabs are now still closed when it wakes, instead of staying open, hidden and muted; you are very unlikely to have seen this.
- **Theme Color menu no longer cut off.** In a narrow side panel, the Theme Color list in a space's right-click menu (and any other sub-menu in Sidecove's menus) now opens on the left, or under its item, so every colour can be seen and clicked. ([#66](https://github.com/matthewmoorcroft/sidecove/issues/66))
- **One list of tabs and favorites across your windows.** Each window's sidebar now shows the current space's open tabs and the top-bar favorites of all your windows, one entry per page, and search finds them too. Which window a tab is in does not show and does not matter: choose a page and it opens in the window you are in. If that window already has it, Sidecove switches to it; if only another window has it, it opens again here and the other window keeps its tab. Closing an entry closes that page in every window, so it leaves the list. Each window keeps its own space, and the next, previous and recent tab shortcuts stay in the window you are in. It is on by default, including after an update; to keep each window to its own tabs as before, turn off Settings → Show tabs and favorites from all windows.
- **README picture in light mode.** The first picture in the README now shows Sidecove in light mode, so the two pictures show both looks. You will not notice this in the extension itself.
- **README spaces picture in light and dark.** The second picture in the README now shows two spaces side by side, one in light mode and one in dark mode, on a soft blue-grey background. You will not notice this in the extension itself.
- **README shows one picture and the logo.** The README now opens with the Sidecove logo and name, centred, and shows a single picture of the sidebar instead of two. You will not notice this in the extension itself.

## 1.3.0

New Tab and Clear now work the way they do in Arc, spaces get their own dark colours, and links, closing tabs and the space bar behave more like Arc too. Optionally, each space can keep its own favorites. No new permissions.

- **New Tab moved under the divider, as in Arc.** The "+ New Tab" row used to sit at the bottom of the panel, below every tab. It now sits directly under the line that separates your pinned tabs from your open tabs, above the open tabs, in each space. Tab order, including the Invert tab order setting, is unchanged. The "Copied" toast now appears just above the space bar.
- **Clean All is now "↓ Clear", as in Arc.** The line between your pinned tabs and your open tabs ends in a small, muted "↓ Clear", directly above New Tab, in place of the "Clean All" button. It closes the open tabs of the space, keeps them in Archived and offers Undo. You can reach it with the keyboard (Tab, then Enter or Space); a screen reader reads it as "Clear open tabs".
- **Undo after Clear brings back every tab, local files included.** Before, if one of the tabs was a file on your computer (and "Allow access to file URLs" is off for Sidecove, as it is by default), Undo stopped at Chrome's message "Cannot navigate to a file URL without local file access" and left the tabs after it in Archived. Clear now takes the tabs out of the list at once and mutes them, but keeps them open for the 8 seconds Undo is on offer. Undo shows the same tabs again with their sound as it was (a tab you had muted yourself stays muted); after 8 seconds Sidecove closes them for good. They are in Archived as before. Closing or reloading the sidebar in those 8 seconds does not leave them hidden.
- **Switching to a space with no open tab opens a New Tab in it, as in Arc.** Before, the panel changed to the empty space but kept the page of the space you came from on screen. Now a New Tab opens in the space you switch to, from the space bar or the keyboard shortcuts. Spaces that have an open tab still show their last tab.
- **New tabs you leave empty close by themselves, as in Arc.** Cmd+T and the "+ New Tab" row work as before, with Chrome's own address bar and your search engine. If you then switch to another tab without typing an address or opening a site, the empty tab closes and you stay on the tab you chose. It never closes a window's last tab, a pinned tab, a favorite, or a tab that has already been to a site. It is on by default; turn it off at Settings → Close new tabs you leave empty. Chrome does not tell extensions about text typed in the address bar but not yet sent, so a tab with only unsent text in it still counts as empty.
- **Dark mode has its own space colours.** In dark mode each space is now a dark surface tinted with its colour (charcoal, navy, wine, olive-brown, forest, plum, purple, teal), instead of the mid-tone colours of before, so the light text is easy to read on every one. Hovered rows are lightened a little so they still show on the darker ground. Settings → Colors now has a Light and a Dark picker for each colour, each with its own Reset, and the sidebar uses the one that matches your system theme. A colour you changed before keeps working as its light value, and a colour with no dark value uses the new built-in dark colour. Light mode looks the same as before.
- **Links open below the tab you clicked them in, in the order you opened them.** A link opened from a tab in the list (Cmd/Ctrl+click, a link that opens a new tab, or a script that opens a window) now lands directly below that tab, and several links in a row stack below each other in the order you opened them. Switch to another tab and come back, and the next link goes directly under that tab, above the earlier ones, as in Chrome. Before, with "Show new tabs at the top" on (the default), the new tab appeared above the tab it came from, and with it off the second and third links came out in reverse order. A link opened from a pinned tab or a favorite goes to the top of the tab list, under the divider. The setting still decides where a New Tab or Cmd/Ctrl+T tab goes. Chrome's own tab strip is not reordered.
- **Closing a tab keeps you in your space.** When you close the tab you are on, Sidecove stays in the same space and shows the next tab down its list (the one above if you closed the last), as Arc does. Before, Chrome could pick a tab from another space, such as the one you had come from, and Sidecove followed it there. If the space has no other tab, it opens a New Tab in that space. Clicking a tab of another space still switches to it. When you pick an open tab from Chrome's address bar suggestions ("Switch to this tab") from an empty New Tab, you stay on the tab you chose.
- **Next and previous tab in space follow the order you see.** With "Show new tabs at the top" on, the two shortcuts walked the open tabs in the opposite direction to the panel. They now go down and up the list as it is shown, favorites first, whichever way new tabs are listed and whether or not spaces sync to Chrome tab groups.
- **A shortcut for the most recently used tab.** A new command, "Switch to the most recently used tab in this space", jumps back to the tab you used last in the current space, as Ctrl+Tab does in Arc. Press it again within about 0.8 seconds to go further back through the tabs you used; each space keeps its own order. Chrome keeps Ctrl+Tab for itself, so the command has no default key: assign one at chrome://extensions/shortcuts. The order is kept in memory for the browser session only.
- **Optional favorites per space.** Turn on Settings → Separate favorites for each space to show only the current space's top-bar favorites. Favorites stay shared across spaces by default. Switching modes keeps your pins and their spaces, including across restarts.
- **The space bar keeps a few spaces together in the middle, as Arc does.** With the icons-only space bar (the default), two or three spaces used to sit at the two ends of the bar with the whole width empty between them. They now sit side by side in the middle. With enough spaces to fill the bar they spread and scroll as before, and the first one is never cut off. The space bar with names (Settings → Space bar shows) is unchanged.
- **Pinned tab matching setting.** Hosts listed under Settings → Pinned tab matching (for example `localhost:6969`) keep their query string when matching pinned tabs, so URLs like `localhost:6969/?focused=<chat id>` pin as one entry per URL instead of collapsing into a single pinned tab. Other hosts keep the old behavior.
- **A new name that is easier to find from Arc.** The extension is now called "Sidecove: Arc-style Spaces", short enough that Chrome's extensions menu does not cut it off. Its description says it brings Arc-style spaces, pinned tabs and favourites to Chrome for people moving from Arc, and the Chrome Web Store text says it is not made by or affiliated with The Browser Company.
- **Smaller download.** The extension package is about 1 MB instead of 2.4 MB. Every image and the font used to ship twice, and an old, unused welcome page and its pictures shipped too; both are gone. Nothing you see changes.
- **Behind the scenes, which you will not notice:** the Chrome Web Store is now the install route in the README, with the unpacked install kept for developers and testers; new issues start from Bug Report and Feature Request forms; the source code moved into `src/`, one folder per part of the extension, with ESLint and Prettier checking it; a release is now a pull request, with the tag made after it merges, and each release publishes a checksum you can verify with `sha256sum -c` and a build provenance record; the end-to-end tests run on the laptop with `npm run test:e2e:local`. The extension's permissions and your data are unchanged.

## 1.2.0

An Arc-style space bar, favorites that only own the tab they opened, a clearer drop indicator and a polish pass, with no new permissions.

- **The space bar works like Arc's.** Each space shows as its icon, or a dot if it has none; the current space is full strength and the name shows on hover, so every space stays in view. Prefer names? Settings → Space bar shows → Names gives full-name chips, which scroll sideways when they don't all fit and fade the edge that has more.
- **Monochrome space symbols, as in Arc.** The icon picker leads with 58 line symbols drawn in one ink tinted by the space colour, with emoji below. Its search box now finds both by name or keyword ("music", "travel", "money"), and a typed emoji still works. The symbols' shapes come from Lucide (ISC licence, see THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md) and ship inside the extension.
- **Switching spaces is instant.** The background and every row take the new space's colours in the same frame, instead of parts of the panel briefly showing as highlighted patches.
- **Right-click a space** in the bar for Change Space Icon, Rename Space, Theme Color, New Folder and Delete Space.
- **A favorite only owns the tab it opened.** Clicking a closed favorite always opens it in its own new tab. A tab you opened yourself is never taken over or linked to a favorite, even when it shows the same page, so two links never show as one favorite.
- **Links from other apps open in the space you're using.** A link opened from Slack, Mail or another app used to land in the first space whenever it arrived in a window Sidecove had no space for yet, such as a second window. It now opens in the space you used last; the Default space setting applies only when there isn't one yet.
- **Collapsed favorites stay hidden.** Collapsing a space's Favorites section used to leave its links invisible but still clickable, so clicking the empty space below could open one, and Tab could still reach them. They now take no clicks or keyboard focus while collapsed.
- **New Tab looks like Arc's:** a plain row with a grey plus and text, no button box, lined up with the tabs and the same height as a tab row.
- **Close buttons follow Arc:** an open favorite shows − (close the tab, keep the favorite); a closed favorite shows × (remove it); ordinary tabs keep ×.
- **Copy current tab URL** now has a default shortcut, ⌥⇧C (Alt+Shift+C). Chrome will not give an extension ⌘⇧C as a default because its developer tools use it, but you can choose it at chrome://extensions/shortcuts; the install guide's Shortcuts step now says so for anyone coming from Arc.
- **Drop indicator:** while you drag a tab, favorite or folder, one line shows exactly where it will land. The line starts at the indent of the level it lands at, and the folder it would go into is highlighted, so a top-level drop and a drop into a nested folder look clearly different. It also shows at the end of a list, where there used to be no line.
- **Favorites grid:** tiles fill the row while there are few and wrap at an even size, so a lone tile on a new row no longer stretches across it (upstream issue #42).
- **Menus:** the tab menu and the space menu share one rounded, shadowed look with tighter rows and a short pop-in. In dark mode the space menu and its colour swatches are dark too.
- **Hover and press:** rows, folder headers, favorites and New Tab respond with a clearer hover and a slight press. In dark mode, hovering a row or folder header darkens it so its title stays readable. The active tab and the active space chip stand out more clearly.
- **Keyboard:** Tab no longer moves focus into the hidden New Space form or archive list, which used to scroll the whole sidebar out of place. Close, remove and folder toggle buttons show when they have keyboard focus.
- Animations respect the system's reduce-motion setting.
- **Guided tour:** Next and the other tour buttons now have light text on a deep fill of the space colour, at least 4.5:1 contrast in every space colour in light and dark, so Next no longer looks disabled. Stepping through the tour no longer flashes a scroll bar under the card.
- The product is called Sidecove throughout, including the tour's "Make Sidecove feel like you". Existing spaces, favorites and settings carry over unchanged.
- The welcome screen points feedback to [GitHub Issues](https://github.com/matthewmoorcroft/sidecove/issues).

## 1.1.0

Closer to Arc, with no new permissions.

- **Switch spaces from the keyboard:** Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+8 for a numbered space, Ctrl+9 for the last one, and Ctrl+Shift+[ or ] to step through them while the sidebar has focus. Next space, Previous space and Switch to space 1-9 can be bound anywhere in Chrome at chrome://extensions/shortcuts.
- **Mouse side buttons** switch spaces from anywhere in the sidebar, not just over the space chips.
- **Search tabs** (Cmd or Ctrl+F): find open tabs, pinned tabs and favorites across every space by title or address.
- **Move things between spaces:** drop a tab, favorite, folder or pinned tab on a space chip.
- **Select several tabs** with Cmd or Ctrl click and Shift click, then move, pin, archive, close or put them in a new folder. Drag a selection to move it together.
- **Make a folder by dropping:** hold a tab over a favorite for a moment and drop it, and both go into a new folder.
- **Tab menu:** Duplicate Tab, Copy Link, Mute or Unmute Tab and Rename Tab.
- **Sound:** tabs that play sound show a speaker; click it to mute or unmute.
- **Space icons:** give each space an emoji. When the chips no longer fit, they shrink to icons or initials, and the active chip stays in view.
- **Clean All** now archives the tabs it closes and offers Undo.
- Custom colours from Settings now show in the colour pickers too.
- The Varela Round font ships with the extension, so the sidebar and settings no longer load it from Google Fonts. Nothing is sent anywhere.

- Removed the unused `scripting` permission. Copy current URL works as before, through Chrome's offscreen clipboard.
